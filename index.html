About

This fishy app was made by Ivan to study vocabulary. Because all flashcard apps that I tried were simply sh*t. Flashker Ultimate is an offline-first vocabulary app for focused learning and review. Organize your own words, choose a study method, and track your progress through a simple interface.
Version 1.0.3. Codename: Flounder. © 2026 Ivan Dimitrov

How to Use

1. Build your vocabulary. Import or add word cards in Vocabulary Decks. You can use copy-paste, csv files, or even AI generation. Then assign tags to organize them into study lists.

2. Choose your mode. Select the method that suits your practice. There are four distinct study modes, which are described below.

3. Start studying. Choose one of your Study Lists, and start learning or reviewing the cards.

4. Use the gestures. Swipe left for Wrong, swipe right for Correct, tap to flip, and long-press to edit.

Try to force yourself to recall the answer before revealing it. Use Undo to reverse your last answer and Quit to finish. Tap the list-name area at the top, or the Previous cards area at the bottom, to hide or restore session details and controls. Both areas remain tappable while hidden.

Choose Voice mode for spoken practice. Flashker reads the prompt, listens in the answer's saved language, and rates your response automatically.

Detailed statistics are available. Your progress follows each word across all its study modes and lists. Export a complete JSON backup from Settings to keep a separate copy of your vocabulary, profiles and learning history.

Study Modes
BASIC (Continuous review)
Simple on-demand free practice of the whole study list. Correct cards wait for the next shuffled round, while wrong cards return much sooner. Choose it for flexible practice or quick review without waiting for due dates or changing your review schedule.
IVAN HCC-SRS (Queue mastery)
Structured practice in small pieces, optionally alternating recall directions and advancing through Leitner boxes. In Flashker, HCC stands for Hierarchical Chunk Consolidation: small card groups are combined into progressively larger review piles. The FSRS formula still calculates next-review due dates, while practice continues independently of them.
SRS (Leitner-inspired spaced repetition)
Box-based Spaced Repetition System with adjustable review intervals and penalties for missed answers. Ten boxes track progress. Correct answers advance cards through Leitner boxes with increasing review intervals; mistakes move them back for additional practice. During an SRS session, select any Leitner box to deliberately review ahead of schedule.
FSRS (Adaptive spaced repetition)
FSRS-6 (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) powers adaptive review scheduling in Anki and other flashcard apps. It uses your recorded recall history and Again, Hard, Good, and Easy ratings to estimate when each card needs another review. This aims to maintain high memory retention with fewer daily reviews than older schedulers such as SM-2. Adjust your desired retention to balance review frequency and workload.

SRS, IVAN and FSRS share recall memory and a single next-review due date; the latest SRS, IVAN or FSRS answer sets that date using its mode. BASIC leaves scheduling unchanged. Local profiles share vocabulary, but keep their own learning records and settings. No account or cloud sync is required.

Food for thought: One problem that I have with other flashcard apps is that they usually schedule words only in terms of minutes or days. I do not find this useful for acquiring brand new vocabulary from scratch because the scheduler will often make you go through the whole list once, and then make you wait until the next review is due. The idea of the IVAN study mode algorithm is to rely on rote memory for initial word acquisition: use high-frequency and high-speed repetition to force automatic recall. How? Try to go through as many cards as quickly as you can! Several times. As always, try to recall the answer before revealing it. My preferred setup is study lists of around 48 words (8 chunks of 6 word cards each). In IVAN study mode, a Wrong first answer reveals the card and forces the user to acknowledge it. Studies demonstrate that reviewing the correct answer after a mistake improves memory retention. Scientific research does not necessarily support or reject many of the other choices for default parameters, such as chunk size and short penalty intervals. But I know that it works for me.

Remember: Leitner box progress reflects practice performance, not permanent mastery — future spaced reviews still matter the most!

Features
Elegant design
An uncluttered interface with touch gestures, smooth navigation and card shortcuts.
Top speed
Very lightweight. The whole app is less than a half of a megabyte. All buttons and gestures work instantly. Fast memorization requires a fast app.
Smart flashcard creation
Add cards manually, paste text, or import CSV and JSON files. Generate vocabulary with AI, review the results, and save the cards you choose. Export decks as CSV whenever you want.
Flexible and painless organization
Arrange decks in folders and subfolders. Use tags to combine words into study lists without duplicating cards or losing your progress.
Your vocabulary. Your pace
Four study modes, including adaptive, Anki-inspired spaced repetition and automatic next-review scheduling.
Practise the other way around
Start from either card face, or let Random choose your starting direction. BASIC and IVAN can alternate directions between rounds or phases.
Make room for concentration
Hide session details and controls for a quieter view; or display comprehensive info, buttons and a list of recent cards. Undo an answer when your finger moves faster than your brain.
See the work adding up
More statistics than you can count. Track answers, accuracy, study time and review history. Set a daily word goal and follow your streaks.
No distractions
No unnecessary social or gamification elements. No highscores, no badges, no coins or gems to collect, no confetti. No misguided motivation: focus on learning, not virtual points. It's just you and your word cards.
Adjustable UI design
Choose light, dark or system theme; adjust typeface, text size and flip effects. Set pronunciation languages for each face and fine-tune your study preferences.
Look, flip, swipe — or speak
Swipe left for Wrong, right for Correct, tap to reveal, and hold to edit. Or press the buttons if you like. Prefer speaking? Switch to Voice Mode.
Study aloud
Hear prompts, answer aloud, and let Flashker check your spoken response against the card. Choose voices, adjust speech speed, and interrupt whenever you want. Voice mode tries to gracefully handle multiple answer options separated by /, | or ;.
Who are you?
Up to ten local profiles share the vocabulary collection while keeping independent progress, preferences and statistics.
Offline study
Study your saved vocabulary offline after the app has loaded and cached successfully. No Flashker account or subscription is required. Export and restore complete JSON backups through local files. Optional AI generation requires internet; speech services may also need it.

And tap the logo when your vocabulary deserves a small explosion.

Options for All Study Modes

BASIC rounds and IVAN phases alternate sides when enabled. Each new IVAN group restarts on the chosen starting side. SRS/FSRS keep one side throughout their queue, including retries. Random is chosen at session start or after a manual box selection. Turning alternation off keeps the chosen starting side.

Options for Study Mode IVAN HCC-SRS Saved per profile; a session uses a frozen copy. Small queues shorten the spacing. Continue grants another answer allowance. IVAN practices the selected list; FSRS schedules its next due dates.
Options for Study Mode SRS
Default: 100%. Range: 50–200%. Scales the existing box intervals: 50% changes 14 days to 7 days; 200% changes it to 28 days. Successful relearning still has a one-day minimum. Default: 2 boxes. Range: 1–3. A larger loss moves the card farther back on its first failure. Immediate relearning stays available; repeated failures during relearning do not subtract more boxes. Default: on. Correct early reviews still advance boxes, but shorten the next interval based on elapsed time. For a 7-day interval normally advancing to 14 days: after 1 day, schedule 7 days; after 5 days, schedule 10 days. Turn off to use the full box interval. Wrong and immediate relearning are unchanged. Saved per profile with Save settings. Changes apply to future SRS answers; existing deadlines and saved boxes are not rewritten.
Options for Study Mode FSRS
Default: 90%. Range: 80–97%. The scheduler aims for this recall rate: higher targets mean more frequent reviews; lower targets mean fewer reviews. This is a target, not a guarantee. Saved per profile with Save settings. Applies to future FSRS and IVAN next-review deadlines because they share FSRS memory. SRS also feeds this memory, but uses its box intervals for the shared deadline. BASIC leaves scheduling unchanged. Existing deadlines are not rewritten. IVAN's untimed practice queue is unchanged.
